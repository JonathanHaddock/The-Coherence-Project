Origin superconductor lab · 2D

Ions on springs, with electrons that actually move through them. An electron pulls nearby ions in. That squeezed patch (red) is extra positive charge, and it can pull in a second electron: the wake behind Cooper pairing. This layer is classical, so it shows the mechanism, not superconductivity itself. The right-hand panel turns the lattice you build into a Tc estimate (Allen–Dynes), scaled from a real metal.

Average coupling λ (all electrons)

–

For each electron: energy with the lattice as it is now, minus with the lattice perfect, divided by its kinetic energy (½ vF²), averaged over all electrons. Positive = the lattice has bent toward the electrons (the glue). Lattice convention λ = Ep/(z t); conventions differ between papers, so compare lab runs with each other, not with a metal's λ.

Pairing signatures (compare with control)

Wake-following: how often an electron passes where another one was a moment earlier, vs chance (1×). The lattice's pull is delayed, so real glue should show a peak at some delay, above the control. Opposite momenta: mean cosine between nearby electrons' directions; 0 = none, negative = they tend to move opposite ways (the k, −k of Cooper pairs). Use 20+ electrons, and run the control to compare.
